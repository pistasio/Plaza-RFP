01

Executive Summary

University dormitories play a vital role in the well-being and the overall satisfaction of a student’s life. However, the current dormitories lack many facilities that provide access to essential services, efficient transportation means and interactive social spaces. This leads to reduced productivity and low student interactions. A survey conducted among Khalifa University students highlighted that 72% prioritized access to a dining restaurant nearby and 67% wanted a 24/7 convenience store along with strong demands for social spaces.

The Agora project proposes redevelopment of the current dormitory facilities in Khalifa University into a multi-functional hub for students to improve convenience, connectivity and social engagement. To address and fill the gaps in improving overall satisfaction, the four components of this proposal are a student social lounge, a dining and convenience store hub, a micro-mobility system and a reward-based reverse vending machines. All these components together contribute to building an environment to improve the academic and personal development of students.

The proposed solution is a structured phased development model that ensures moderate financial feasibility and scalability while having low disruption to the current dormitory infrastructure. Once the solution is implemented, few of the expected outcomes are increased student satisfaction, better time efficiency, improved social interactions and higher engagement in sustainable activities through the reward-based recycling systems.

By aligning with Khalifa University’s long-term vision and mission, The Agora project provides a practical solution to rebuild the current dormitory into an efficient and inclusive environment for students.

02

Introduction

The Agora Project proposes the transformation of the newly announced plaza spaces within Khalifa University’s new dormitories [1]. It aims to convert the empty spaces into a cozy, multifunctional community hub. While these areas were originally envisioned as a social space, they lack the right infrastructure to support student engagement. This proposal aims to change that by introducing four features: a community engagement lounge, a dining and convenience store hub, a sustainable micro-mobility station, and reverse vending machines. Together, these elements aim to bring the university closer to its five strategic goals 2024-2028 [2].

03

Literature Review

Studies have shown that student accommodation and its environment play a significant role in the academic growth, wellbeing and overall university satisfaction of the students. Poorly designed dormitories, especially the ones that lack convenient services and social lounges, directly or indirectly increase stress, cause isolation and lower interactions among students [3],[4]. This suggests that besides the basic living needs, students need a friendly and convenient environment that supports their academic and personal development.

A key factor that was found in many studies was the significance of social spaces. Spaces like a lounge along with the student housing have shown increase in peer interactions, formation of a sense of belonging and overall wellbeing [5]. Additionally, access to convenient services like a supermarket or a restaurant influences the daily routine of students and their satisfaction with campus life [6],[7]. When such services are barred for students, they experience inconvenience and reduced productivity.

Furthermore, studies on university campus mobility points out that large university environments can bring challenges in accessibility. So, solutions like e-scooters have been studied and suggest that it improves travel efficiency and saves time as well [8]. Having such a transportation system enhances mobility and campus connectivity.

Finally, research on sustainability implies that incentive-based recycling systems like reverse vending machines increase recycling motives in students. Studies show that if rewards are given for participation in such initiatives, it encourages more students to have a recycling mindset to return recyclable materials [9]. All these findings together support the need for improvement in the dormitory while considering convenience, mobility, social interactions and sustainability.

04

Survey Analysis

To establish the need for this project, we conducted a survey to identify current problems in the dorms. The results show that most of the students order food from delivery apps daily. 66% of the students reported ordering food at least 4 to 6 times a week. This high percentage shows that the current options in the dormitories do not meet daily needs. When we asked them to choose their top priorities from a list including Upgraded lounges, rentable e-scooters and 24/7 convenience stores, a Restaurant/Dining hall was the top request, chosen by 72% of all participants, followed closely by the 24/7 Convenience Store at 67%.

Regarding student facilities, the data shows that private study rooms and entertainment options like pool tables or gaming setups are a priority, with over 60% of students selecting them as a must-have lounge upgrade. There were other suggestions like improving the gym facilities and better cleaning services to maintain a better living standard in the dorm. These statistics prove that our proposal is based on the preferences of the students living in these dorms. By focusing on these specific resources, the project can deliver a better environment that can help reduce inconveniences and study issues students face every day.

05

Project Description

The Agora Project is an initiative to seek to reconfigure the recently announced public spaces in the new dorm buildings at Khalifa University [10]. It aims to convert the under-used areas into a friendly community centre. It is a space designed for public use, but which does not have the means to be used by students, which risks and contributes to disengagement. The plan includes several features: a community engagement lounge, a dining and convenience store, a sustainable micro-mobility station, and reverse vending machines. These additions will support the university's goals for 2024-2028, such as enhancing student experience, promoting sustainability, and fostering innovation [11].

This project will take place in two phases to develop inviting and dynamic plazas. One of the major components of the redesign is the Community Engagement Lounge. This area will be used for academic learning, socialization, and will be inviting for all. The lounge will be a place that students will regularly use for studying, working and socialising. It will feature adaptable furnishings, shade, lighting, and power outlets. The design seeks to create a sense of community and belonging within the dormitory, by promoting a welcoming and accessible environment.

The Dining and Convenience Store Hub will be free for all and will feature a range of food and other provisions. It will offer a fast and inexpensive place to snack and get your meals. Students will be able to pay using digital currency and thus, they can work longer hours and can manage the payment of their meal providing a convenient handling. This will enhance the plaza's pedestrian-friendly nature and attract more people to use this space.

Furthermore, a micro mobility station will be set up to enhance the mobility on the campus and for sustainable mobility. This station will feature e-scooter and bike parking, charging stations and other amenities. Student will be able to navigate an easy-to-use mobile application for short-distance transportation. The project will not only decrease the reliance on cars but will also promote sustainable transportation, contributing to the overall sustainability objectives.

They also have a reverse vending machine (RVM) as part of the project to support their sustainability goals. Students are encouraged to participate by earning rewards through an incentive based recycling program for recycling plastic bottles and cans. Evidence indicates that incentive programs are more effective than traditional programs in promoting reuse and recycling [12]. In addition, RVMs in campus settings have also been found to be effective in raising awareness and convenience [11].

Recent improvements in smart recycling technology will enable the system to be further enhanced by incorporating AI for more accurate detection and IoT integration, that will make the whole process even more efficient and convenient. For example, high-tech RVMs feature computer vision, sensors and other technologies embedded within to rapidly recognize recyclables and connect them to rewards [13]. The high accuracy of these systems in detection and short processing time make them attractive within the University and thus promote sustainability [13]. RVMs will be available around the campus in a prominent location, particularly in the area of the dining hub, and will also feature a rewards system online to ensure that they are seen and used regularly.

In general, the Agora Project establishes a fully integrated multi-functional hub of a campus which integrates the social, functional and environmental aspects. These elements all help to create a stimulating environment for students to learn, socialize, use services, and participate in sustainable activities. This revitalization will not only free up space, but also make the students feel more at home and supported at the university and will enable Khalifa University to be an innovative and exemplary university.

06

Expected Challenges

One major challenge we could face with this project is costs. Everyone would like all these features to be implemented into the new accommodation, however the budget may not be enough to fulfill all the proposed plans, and would require more than one phase to complete. This accommodation is also being marketed as the GCC’s largest student accommodation yet, and living up to that title means that maintenance and operation cost could be very high. Managing a large scale accommodation with multiple services requires continuous funding and staff, and if done incorrectly, can lead to a decline in facilities and student’s daily lives.

Another challenge could be the noise and distractions students may face with these new facilities. With these new facilities such as the lounges, sports areas and social spaces, students could be facing an issue of balancing social life and academic focus, as the implementation of shared spaces encourages more interactions within students, which may lead to an excessive amount of social activity that distract students from their work and could increase stress in the long term if this repeats frequently.

Another challenge we could face is overcrowding and the unequal usage of facilities. Some of the spaces such as gyms or lounges may become overcrowded during peak times, while other facilities may be unused which leads to an inefficiency in using resources. To face this, we could publish polls and surveys for students living in the dorms to help us decide which facilities are the most important in their day to day lives, and allocate the resources accordingly.

07

Conclusion

In conclusion, The Agora Project isn’t about filling up empty spaces, it’s about making the space meaningful and effective, while directly contributing to Khalifa University’s goals and missions of being a more sustainable and innovative institution. By introducing retail stores, cost efficient cafeterias, green transport, and reverse vending machines, these plazas will become an active part of student life. Overall, this proposal is in line with Khalifa University’s vision for innovation and its 2024-2028 strategic goals, while providing a seamless and joyful life for the students in dorms.
